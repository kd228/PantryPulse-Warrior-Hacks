# PantryPulse pitch deck outline (12 slides)

| # | Slide | Key message |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Cover | PantryPulse: real-time matching of surplus food to the pantries that need it most |
| 2 | Problem | Food is thrown out in one part of town while a pantry across town runs short. USDA estimates 30–40% of the US food supply is wasted. Two causes: timing and mismatch. |
| 3 | Who it affects | Donors (no time to call around), pantries (limited storage, surprise deliveries), families (miss fresh produce and dairy) |
| 4 | Our idea | A matching layer in three steps: Post, Match, Dispatch |
| 5 | How it works (flowchart) | Donor posts → safety filters → score → split and send → confirm and log. No eligible pantry alerts the donor. Need changes re-rank immediately. |
| 6 | Matching logic | Hard filters first (cold storage, arrives in time, arrives before closing, has space), then a score: 40% need, 25% distance, 20% time to spare, 15% space |
| 7 | Decision assistant | Instant answers from the engine plus typed questions to Claude. It advises, the coordinator decides, and it never rules on food safety. |
| 8 | Working prototype | Sample run: 180 lb produce, 4 hr window. Eastside ranks first (90.5). Riverbend is ruled out for no cold storage. |
| 9 | Impact | Pounds rerouted, meals provided (pounds ÷ 1.2), time to match, need fit |
| 10 | Feasibility | Risks and plans: food safety, stale data, adoption, privacy, assistant errors |
| 11 | Roadmap | Six-week pilot, then volunteer routing, then county scale |
| 12 | The ask | Pilot PantryPulse with a local food network |

Sources: USDA Food Loss and Waste FAQ; Feeding America, About Our Claims.
